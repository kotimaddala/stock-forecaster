# Scanner backtest (point-in-time)

Generated by `python -m eval.backtest_scanner --start 2024-01-01` in Google Colab on 2026-11-20,
on the default 30-ticker universe with daily prices. Each day the scanner sees only past data.

| Measure | Result |
| --- | --- |
| Trades | 135 |
| Win rate | 42% |
| Average result per trade (in units of risk, R) | 0.11 |
| Average trade return | 0.45% |

Costs, slippage and taxes are not modeled; results are directional, not proof.

## Reading the results

- The scanner made money on paper (+0.11 R per trade, about 6% on the account over the period),
  but its average trade return (0.45%) matched simply holding SPY over the same days (0.46%).
  It shows no edge over the index, and trading costs would push it below.
- A 42% win rate with positive R is what a stop-and-target design should produce: losses are
  cut at the stop and winners run to a target about twice as far away.
- The risk controls held: maximum drawdown stayed under 9% with 1% risk per trade.
- This is why every scanner proposal requires human approval, and why the thesis reviewer, not
  the scanner, is the core of the project.
